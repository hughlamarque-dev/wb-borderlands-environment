Data sources, licences and limitations

Data sources, licences and limitations

This map combines third-party geospatial datasets that have been clipped, filtered, symbolised, aggregated or transformed for cartographic presentation.

Development projects

IATI records were retrieved through d-portal. The four priority groups are World Bank, EU institutions, German development cooperation including GIZ, and FCDO. Additional large institutional programmes are screened at USD 5 million in converted commitments, or on explicit borderland relevance. Amounts are screening aids, not local allocations. Activities ending before 2020 are generally outside this release; missing dates do not prove current activity.

The seven working areas follow Sam Sager’s 8 September 2026 register and roadmap. Exact official-ID matches inherit that coding. Other tags are provisional narrative and DAC-code classifications. One project may contribute to several working areas. Unclassified records remain available.

Published coordinates are not independently verified field sites. Exact named administrative-area matches use a representative point within the area’s intersection with the cluster, shown as a hollow diamond. Country-wide or regional projects without local evidence remain in the directory. Marker clusters count unique project families, and explicit same-publisher parent/child declarations group related records. Cross-publisher duplicates can remain. Absence of a mapped project is not proof of an investment gap. Filtered CSVs can be downloaded after unlocking the site.

General limitations

The datasets come from different institutions, dates, methods and spatial resolutions. They form a layered evidence base rather than a single authoritative dataset. Point features may be incomplete, misclassified, duplicated or out of date. Raster and hex layers are modelled or aggregated indicators and should not be interpreted as exact values at individual locations.

Analytical indicators. Population-weighted healthcare bands are estimates produced by aligning the WorldPop 2025 and Malaria Atlas Project 2019 rasters at approximately their native 1 km scale, then aggregating population-cell values into 10 km analysis hexes. The launcher also aggregates healthcare and ACLED time series into cluster-clipped countries and Borderland Districts; these are not full national or administrative-area totals. Borderland Districts use the finest named district geography available in the source and fall back to the next available district level where necessary. Persistent violence records whether at least one included ACLED event occurred in each of the five August-aligned observation periods covering 1 August 2020–1 August 2025. Violence displays include actual recorded totals and population-adjusted rates. The rates divide the five-period totals by five and express the annual average per 10,000 WorldPop-estimated residents; they are rates, not event counts, and can remain volatile where estimated populations are very small.
